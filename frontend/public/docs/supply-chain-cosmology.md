# Supplier Cosmology Map

The cosmology map is the big-picture view of how all your suppliers relate to one another — which imprints belong to which publishers, who distributes whom, and which vendor codes are legacy leftovers you shouldn't use anymore.

It's a read-only reference. You can't change anything here; it's for understanding and looking things up. (Editing will come in a later version.)

---

## The two tabs

### Tree view
Shows the hierarchy. Parent publishers expand to reveal their imprints and the independent publishers they distribute. This is how you see, at a glance, that Viking and Knopf sit under Penguin Random House, or which small publishers a distributor handles.

### Code Lookup
A searchable list for answering "what is this code?" Type a vendor code or a name and it tells you what it maps to and how it relates to everything else. Handy when a book has a cryptic code on it and you need to know who it really comes from.

---

## The relationship types

Every entry on the map is tagged with how you relate to it. The colors and labels mean:

- **Ordering party** — you write purchase orders directly to this party. These are your real ordering points.
- **Imprint** — an editorially distinct brand under a parent. Orders route through the parent, not the imprint.
- **Distribution client** — an independent publisher distributed *through* a larger party. You order via the distributor, not the publisher directly.
- **Direct** — ordered directly, with no distributor or rep group in between.
- **Deprecated** — a legacy code from the old Booklog system. **Do not use these for new orders.** They're kept so old records still make sense, not for going forward.

---

## What this is good for

- Working out **who you actually order from** when a book is branded with an imprint's name.
- Decoding a **vendor code** you don't recognize.
- Understanding why a title routes the way it does — through a parent or a distributor rather than the name on the cover.
- Spotting **deprecated codes** so you steer clear of them on new orders.

---

## How this connects to ordering

The map explains the structure; the Suppliers and Purchase Orders pages are where you act on it. If the map shows an imprint routes through a parent, that's your cue to place the PO against the parent. If it shows a code is deprecated, that's your cue to find the current party instead.

---

## Do's & Don'ts

**Do**
- Use **Code Lookup** when you hit an unfamiliar code on a book or slip.
- Trust the **routing** the map shows — order through the parent or distributor it points to.
- Treat **Deprecated** codes as off-limits for anything new.

**Don't**
- Don't expect to edit here — it's a reference view for now.
- Don't place new orders against a deprecated code just because old records use it.

---

## Quick reference

- **Ordering party** — someone you write POs to directly.
- **Imprint** — a brand under a parent; orders route through the parent.
- **Distribution client** — an independent publisher handled through a distributor; order via the distributor.
- **Direct** — ordered straight from the source, no middleman.
- **Deprecated** — a legacy code kept for old records; never use for new orders.
