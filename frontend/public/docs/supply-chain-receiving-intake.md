# Receiving — Intake

This is the start of every receiving session. Its job is to figure out *which order* the box in front of you belongs to, and then hand you off to count the books in.

There are two ways through: scan the packing slip and let the system match it, or pick the order yourself. Scanning is the fast path and handles the hard cases — multi-page slips, and single shipments that cover several orders at once.

---

## Step 1: Scan the packing slip

You'll land on the scan screen. Tap **Add page 1** to photograph the slip.

### Getting a good scan

The quality of the scan decides how much you'll have to fix by hand afterward, so it's worth getting right.

- **Use your phone's built-in document scan feature for the best results.** On most phones the Notes or Files app has a "Scan Document" mode that straightens the page, sharpens the text, and drops out the background. A scan made that way reads far more reliably than a plain photo — reach for it first.
- If you're taking a plain photo instead: lay the slip **flat**, hold the phone **directly above** it in **portrait** orientation, get **all four edges** in the frame, and make sure the lighting is even with **no glare or shadow** across the text.
- **One page per photo.** Don't try to fit two pages into one shot.

### Multi-page slips

Many slips run more than one page — RDH slips routinely come as two. Add them one at a time: scan page 1, tap **Add page** for page 2, and so on. Each page shows as a thumbnail with its own status while the system reads it. When every page is in, tap **Process**.

The system reads all the pages and merges them into a single list of titles and quantities before it does anything else.

### Looking it up by hand

Prefer to choose the order yourself? You can skip scanning and use the lookup to filter the receivable orders and pick the right one directly.

---

## Step 2: Check the lines it read

After processing, you'll see the list of titles the scan pulled off the slip. **This is your chance to fix anything the scan misread before it's used to match an order.**

- Each line shows its ISBN, title, and quantity, with a confidence badge. Lines the system is unsure about are flagged for review.
- **You can edit any ISBN or title directly.** If a digit was misread, correct it — a corrected line is marked so you can see what you changed. Fixing an ISBN here is what lets it match the right order in the next step.
- Adjust quantities if they don't match what the slip says.

Take a moment here. A misread ISBN that slips through just means more to untangle later.

---

## Step 3: Confirm where it's going

Once the lines look right, the system works out which order (or orders) the slip belongs to, and shows you what it found so you can **confirm before anything is received**. It matches two ways:

1. **By reference** — it reads the slip's text for a PO number or reference.
2. **By ISBN** — it compares the books on the slip against your open orders.

Whatever it finds, you get a confirmation screen rather than being sent off automatically. That screen will show one of:

- **A single order** — the slip matches one open PO. Confirm to receive against it, or override if it's wrong.
- **Several orders (a multi-PO slip)** — see below.
- **An unsure list** — a few possible orders ranked by how well they fit. Pick the right one.
- **No match** — nothing lined up; you can send it to the ad hoc path or pick an order by hand.

You're always the one who decides. If the system's best guess is wrong, override it right here.

---

## Multi-PO slips: one box, several orders

A single carton from a distributor often fills **several of your POs at once** — a Hachette box might carry titles from a Hachette order, an Abrams order placed through Chesapeake & Hudson, and a Chronicle order, all together. The system recognizes this and gives you a **dashboard** for the shipment.

- Each order that the slip touches gets its **own card**, showing how many of its lines are on this slip.
- **Receive them in any order.** Tap a card to receive that PO; the dashboard stays put and tracks your progress ("2 of 3 received · 1 pending") so nothing gets dropped.
- **Each order gets its own receipt** — the books are split back to the right POs even though they came in one box.
- Titles on the slip that don't belong to any open order land in an **unassigned** group at the bottom — mis-ships, new titles, or items on an order that hasn't been entered. Review those separately; they aren't received here.

When you open a card to receive it, you count in that order's books the same way you would any single shipment. Lines the slip didn't carry show as **"Not on slip"** — leave them, or mark a supply status on them (see below).

---

## Supply status: when something didn't ship

Slips routinely tell you a title *didn't* come — backordered, out of stock, out of print. Recording that keeps the order honest about what's still expected versus what's never coming.

You can mark a supply status two ways:

- **While receiving** — on a "Not on slip" line in the receive panel, mark it without entering any quantity.
- **Any time, from the order itself** — open the PO from Purchase Orders and use the **"supply issue?"** link on any line that hasn't fully arrived. This is the one to use when a publisher emails or calls about a title outside of a receiving session.

The three statuses:

- **Backordered** — the supplier has it and will ship later. The line stays open, waiting for that shipment.
- **Out of stock** — temporarily unavailable, no date given. Also stays open.
- **Out of print** — it's not coming. This **closes the line** — it won't be expected in future shipments.

You can add a short note alongside any of them ("supplier said Q4 restock," "discontinued per Hachette rep"), and you can change or clear a status later as the situation changes.

---

## When there's no matching order (ad hoc)

Sometimes a shipment arrives with no PO — a direct delivery, a surprise reorder, something never entered. That's an **ad hoc** receipt:

1. **Identify the supplier** — tell the system who it's from.
2. **Enter the lines** — add each title and quantity (the scan can pre-fill these).
3. **Review the summary** — on confirm, a purchase order is created for you from what you entered, and you're taken in to receive against it.

So even with no paperwork in the system, you end up with a proper order on record.

---

## New titles you've never carried

If a book on the slip isn't in the catalog yet, you can create it on the spot. It pre-fills what it can read from the slip (ISBN, title, cost, supplier) and you fill in the rest. Once created, it's added to the session like any other line.

---

## Do's & Don'ts

**Do**
- Use your phone's document scan feature — it reads far better than a plain photo.
- Add multi-page slips one page at a time before processing.
- Fix misread ISBNs and titles on the review screen *before* confirming the match.
- On a multi-PO slip, work the cards until the tally shows everything handled.
- Mark backordered / out of stock / out of print so the order reflects what's actually still coming.

**Don't**
- Don't fight a bad photo — rescan it, ideally with the document scanner.
- Don't accept a review line you know is misread; correct it first.
- Don't guess at a match. If the confirmation screen looks wrong, override it.
- Don't abandon a box with no PO — ad hoc receiving exists exactly for that.

---

## Quick reference

- **Packing slip** — the document in the box listing what shipped.
- **Document scan** — your phone's built-in scanner (Notes/Files); straightens and sharpens the page for the most reliable read.
- **Routing confirmation** — the screen where you confirm or override which order(s) a slip belongs to before receiving.
- **Multi-PO slip** — one shipment that fills several of your orders; each is received separately from a shared dashboard.
- **Supply status** — a note on a line that didn't ship: backordered, out of stock, or out of print.
- **Ad hoc receipt** — receiving something with no existing PO; the system creates one for you.
- **ISBN match** — matching a slip to an order by comparing the books on it.
