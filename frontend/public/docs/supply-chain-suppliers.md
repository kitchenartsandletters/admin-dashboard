# Suppliers

This is the directory of everyone you order books from — publishers, distributors, wholesalers, small presses, and individual authors. It's where you look people up, see how to order from them, and add new ones.

---

## How a supplier is organized

Each supplier is made of a few parts, and it helps to know the vocabulary:

- **Party** — the organization itself (e.g. "Penguin Random House," "Chronicle Books"). This is the top-level entry.
- **Account** — *how* you order from that party: the account number, the ordering method, freight terms, and so on. A party can have more than one account.
- **Contact** — a person there: name, email, phone, role.

Most of the time you'll think in terms of the party. The account and contact details live inside it.

---

## Roles (the filter tabs)

Suppliers are tagged with one or more roles, and the tabs across the top filter by them:

- **Distributors** — companies that distribute many publishers' books (e.g. a distribution center).
- **Wholesalers** — stock-holding middlemen you can order from for fast restocks.
- **Publishers** — the publishing houses themselves.
- **Small Press** — smaller independent publishers.
- **Direct** — sources you order from directly, with no distributor or rep group in between.
- **Authors** — individual authors you buy from directly.

There are also **All**, **Active**, and **Draft** views. Your tab choice is remembered between visits.

A single supplier can wear more than one hat — a party might be both a publisher and someone you order from directly.

---

## Imprints and parents

Big publishers contain many imprints (Viking, Knopf, Clarkson Potter, and so on, all under Penguin Random House). In the directory these show up as children of their parent. You generally **order through the parent**, even though a book might be branded with the imprint's name. The detail panel for a parent shows its imprints; opening one shows who its parent is.

If you want the full picture of how everything relates — who's an imprint, who's a distribution client, which codes are legacy — that's what the **Cosmology Map** is for. See its guide.

## Finding a supplier

Use the search box to filter by name or role. It searches as you type.

---

## Opening a supplier

Click any row to open its detail panel. You'll see its roles, its imprints or children if it has any, its accounts, and contacts. From here you can also start a **New PO** to that supplier, or edit its details.

---

## Adding or editing a supplier

The supplier form opens as a single scrollable window with three collapsible sections:

1. **Party** — the name, legal name, roles, parent (if it's an imprint of something), country, website, and payment terms.
2. **Account** — a label, account number, how you order (email, EDI, portal, phone), the ordering email or URL, and freight terms.
3. **Contact** — a person's name, title, role, email, and phone.

You don't have to fill in everything. A party with just a name and a role is a valid start; you can add account and contact details later.

---

## A note on vendor codes and "SMP"

Books carry short vendor codes inherited from the old system (like HAR, WOR, TSP). The system knows how these map to the real suppliers, so ordering and receiving keep working whether a book is labeled with a code or a full name.

One to be aware of: **SMP** is a generic, low-information catch-all that's been overused over the years ("I didn't know what code to use, so I used SMP"). It doesn't reliably tell you who the real supplier is. When you're setting up or correcting a supplier, prefer the specific, correct party over a vague code like SMP.

---

## Do's & Don'ts

**Do**
- Think in terms of the **party** first; account and contact details live inside it.
- Order through the **parent** publisher for imprint titles unless you know otherwise.
- Start a supplier with just a name and role if that's all you have — fill in the rest later.

**Don't**
- Don't treat **SMP** as meaningful supplier information — it's a vague catch-all, not a real identification.
- Don't create a duplicate party for an imprint that should sit under an existing parent. Check first.

---

## Quick reference

- **Party** — the supplier organization itself.
- **Account** — how you order from a party (account number, method, freight). A party can have several.
- **Contact** — a person at the supplier.
- **Role** — what the supplier is to you: distributor, wholesaler, publisher, small press, direct, or author.
- **Imprint** — a brand under a larger publisher; you usually order through the parent.
- **Vendor code** — a short legacy label on a book (HAR, WOR, …) that maps to a real supplier.
